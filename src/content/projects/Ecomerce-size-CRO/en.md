## Context

For this project, the UX team was tasked with investigating sales underperformance in the Crocs e-commerce store, as data indicated a bottleneck between total session traffic, purchase intent sessions (products added to cart), and reaching the checkout.


### Stakeholder Needs

Stakeholders required identifying the friction points preventing users from completing purchases and finding potential optimizations in the shopping experience.

## Research

### Understanding Data and User Behavior

To truly understand the problem, it was necessary to understand user behavior on the website, leading to a two-pronged investigation:

1. **Navigation data analytics:** A quantitative analysis was conducted on traffic data from the previous month across platforms connected to the e-commerce store, such as Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity.

   * **The digital channel barrier:** Users preferred buying in physical stores because customer service, personalized attention, and product tangibility were far superior to what the website currently offered.

2. **The voice of the user:** A plan was designed and executed where data was collected through a series of surveys strategically placed throughout the funnel to capture user frustrations, pain points, and needs at each stage of the purchase process.

   * **Post-purchase frustrations:** The first survey was activated to evaluate whether user recall could indicate the moment of highest friction experienced during the journey. It was found that customers placed higher importance on incentives (e.g., discounts, free gifts, or any incentive that helped or made finalizing their purchase special).
   * **Unresolved checkout needs:** Users found promotional discounts by reference confusing, expressing that prices seemed to change because the products they selected did not qualify for the promotions they expected.
   * **Unresolved cart friction:** Users reaching the cart did not find enough convenience to complete their purchase; while payment methods were functional, they lacked an expected value-add, as users wished for ongoing discounts at checkout. Others sought payment flexibility, such as better installment options, and finally, some felt hesitant due to limited product information to a lesser degree.
   * **Doubts when adding a product:** The overwhelming majority found Crocs sizing very confusing, expressing high dissatisfaction when trying to figure out which size to choose. Even though the size guide answered measurement questions, this section proved to be the primary pain point, matching the drop-off data identified in the quantitative funnel analysis.

## What Was Done

### Structuring and Solution

With clear research insights, an action plan was defined across two fronts:

1. **Optimize the size selection process:**
   - Clearly present and improve the way the size guide is displayed.
   - Facilitate measurement recognition for each size, reducing error margins when selecting a size.
   - Standardize to a single size display system, eliminating confusion caused by multiple standards in favor of the EUR standard only.

2. **Facilitate footwear fit identification:**
   - Create a tag system that makes it easy to identify wide, roomier, or narrow fits depending on the Crocs model, facilitating proper size selection.


### Proposal Validation

The solutions were first validated through testing with proxy users whose demographics matched the survey respondents. After refining weaknesses in the proposal, a production test was launched to monitor return rate indicators directly.

## Results

The switch to the EUR sizing system (implemented in February) was a measurable success across multiple dimensions:

1. **Size-related return rate:** Product returns due to sizing dropped by -11.38%, shifting from an index of 13.27% (Nov–Jan) → *11.76% (Feb–Apr)*.
2. **Clicks on size selector:** +33% (from 24,425 to 32,893 clicks in the same period).
3. **Rage clicks:** -14% (0.43% → 0.37% in the same period).
4. **Dead clicks:** -14% (14.71% → 12.71% in the same period).


## Key Learnings

Global sizing standards that work in other markets must be localized to the target market's needs, as minor size variations can create significant differences in user perception, substantially impacting conversion rates, product returns, and overall CRO.
