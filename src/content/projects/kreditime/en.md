## Context

Kreditime is a financial platform offering purchase credit in up to three interest-free installments, with payment protection applied at the time of purchase. The product aimed to enable a customizable financing model across digital channels and physical stores.

I joined to accelerate the user experience and interface design. The scope was still taking shape, and work continued through the first year. Later, a budget reduction led the team to narrow the scope and prepare an MVP in under six months with a new development team.

## Challenge

The project needed to turn a credit offer into a clear, trustworthy experience, support a faster approval process, and connect different sales channels. At the same time, we needed to organize product needs and reduce scope changes that delayed production releases.

Identity verification was a particularly sensitive moment: it needed to build confidence for both the person applying for credit and the store associate helping them.

## Research

I combined qualitative and quantitative methods to understand user needs, friction points, and the service's operational context:

- We tested proxy applications with six participants to identify strengths and weaknesses in the flows.
- We held short interviews about needs related to fast credit.
- We reviewed approval and rejection rates for credit applications previously handled by third parties.
- We measured process times across different stages of the customer journey.
- We created an empathy map and *How Might We* questions to organize user motivations and concerns.

Tests with wireframes and early screens highlighted the importance of making identity document capture and inspection clearer, especially for the Colombian national ID card.

## Process and solution

I organized the design work into cycles of research, ideation, prototyping, testing, and iteration. We defined the information architecture and flows for external and internal users, and structured the product into three platforms: a website, a CRM, and self-service tools for customers and merchants.

The MVP plan included three deliverables: a website, a registration and self-service system, and a basic CRM for managing customer credit and payments. The architecture covered public and account information for customers; internal tools for administration, customer service, and portfolio and risk analysis; and merchant features such as credit applications, status checks, sales registration, and reporting.

I iterated on document capture and inspection modules and structured a UI Kit using Atomic Design principles. The component system aimed to make the interface more consistent, support reuse across modules, and help the new development team continue the work.

In my Project Manager and Product Owner role, I introduced sprints to prioritize deliverables and coordinate work, helping contain change requests that affected progress toward production.

## Results

- The platform registered more than 500 users in its first two months of operation.
- In February 2024, Kreditime reached 10% of Kronotime e-commerce's monthly revenue.
- The service was deployed in 16 Invicta watch stores in Colombia.
- The Design Thinking approach and UI Kit gave the team a shared foundation for developing key MVP modules.

## Learnings

Defining and prioritizing scope early is essential for maintaining development momentum, especially when a product depends on multiple teams and channels. A reusable visual system supports continuity, but it needs clear product decisions and regular validation cycles alongside it.
