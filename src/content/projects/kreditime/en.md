## Context

Kreditime was a financial platform that offered credit for purchases. The product aimed to enable a customizable financing model that could operate in both digital channels and physical stores. It was also designed to support the operation of the company's e-commerce sites and strengthen its business ecosystem. The platform was discontinued in 2024.

## Role

I led the Kreditime project as a product designer. During my time on the project, I defined the scope of the features and made sure they stayed aligned with business goals. I also managed stakeholder relationships and progress presentations, and carried out the UX research, UI design, and usability testing to improve the experience for users.

## Challenge

The main challenge was to restart a project that had gone 18 months without deliverables and push its launch in record time. Together with the development team, we built a functional MVP for customers on an e-commerce website in under six months, despite an unexpected budget cut.

## Research

During research, we used qualitative methods, such as interviews with store sales associates, and quantitative methods, such as surveys on the company's e-commerce sites. The goal was to identify which features and needs to prioritize. Because the timeline was short, we had to focus our resources on the aspects that would make the platform useful and appealing to customers.

In usability tests of competitors' platforms, we found that the onboarding and credit management flows raised the most questions. Onboarding included both the customer flow and the flow for internal team members who verified customers' identity. For that reason, we defined that the MVP had to cover both platforms around that process.

Research activities included:

- We ran usability tests with eight participants on reference apps (Addi and Sistecrédito) to identify strengths and weaknesses in their onboarding flows.
- We briefly interviewed potential customers and sales associates in the company's stores about their needs around fast credit.
- We ran card sorting activities with company employees who had recently held active credit, to identify an information architecture that met the needs of credit users.

## Process and solution

I led the design work in cycles of research, ideation, prototyping, testing, and iteration. This process helped us find solutions for each section of the platforms more quickly.

Three deliverables were defined for the MVP: a website, a registration and self-service system, and a basic CRM to manage credit and payments.

We defined the information architecture based on what we learned from card sorting, which helped make the interface more intuitive.

The customer account included an available balance area, a section to manage credit, and another to edit contact details.

The internal tools were organized around three roles: administration, customer service, and portfolio and risk analysis. From the interviews, we identified the sections each role needed, such as credit applications, status checks, sales registration, and reports.

Testing and research showed that identity verification was a sensitive moment and had to convey security, clarity, and speed. For that reason, we optimized that part of the process several times: the intermediate designs did not make clear which stage the person was in or what they needed to do to complete verification correctly. The next version was the one that performed best.

At the end of the design and iteration process, we created a UI Kit based on Atomic Design principles. The component system aimed to give the interface consistency, make reuse across modules easier, and support the continuity of the work with the new development team. It also made it possible to redesign only the modules that had problems.

## Results

- The platform registered more than 500 users in its first two months of operation.
- In February 2024, Kreditime reached 10% of the monthly revenue of the Kronotime.com e-commerce site.
- The service was deployed in 16 Invicta watch stores in Colombia.
- The Design Thinking approach and the UI Kit gave the team a shared foundation for developing the key MVP modules.

## Learnings

Defining and prioritizing scope early is essential for maintaining development momentum, especially when a product depends on multiple teams and channels and there is a demanding deadline. A reusable visual system that is easy to iterate on supports continuity, but it needs clear product decisions and regular validation cycles alongside it.
